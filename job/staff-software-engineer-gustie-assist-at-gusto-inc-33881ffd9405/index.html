About the job

About Gusto

At Gusto, we're on a mission to grow the small business economy. We handle the hard stuff — payroll, health insurance, 401(k)s, and HR — so owners can focus on their craft and their customers. With teams in Denver, San Francisco, and New York, we support more than 500,000 small businesses nationwide and are building a workplace that reflects the people we serve.

All full-time employees receive competitive base pay, benefits, and equity (RSUs) — because everyone who helps build Gusto should share in its success. Offer amounts are determined by role, level, and location. Learn more about our Total Rewards philosophy.

AI is a fundamental part of how work gets done at Gusto. We expect all team members to actively engage with AI tools relevant to their role and grow their fluency as the technology evolves. AI experience requirements vary by role and will be assessed during the interview process.

Gustie Assist builds Otto, the AI agent that Gusto's support advocates rely on to solve customers' hardest payroll, benefits, and compliance problems. When a small business hits a wall and reaches out, the advocate turns to Otto: it reasons over the customer's full context, answers in the moment, and increasingly takes real actions across Gusto's systems on the advocate's behalf to make the customer whole. We're a small platform team building an agent that reasons, acts, and has to get it right when the stakes are someone's payroll.

This is unusually broad, hands-on applied-AI work: you'll own an LLM agent runtime and the full stack around it, including the tool layer that lets it act over Gusto's systems, the Rails/GraphQL backend, the advocate-facing frontend, and the eval system that keeps it trustworthy while it touches live customer accounts. We keep the path from idea to production short, and measure ourselves on customer outcomes, not model novelty.

Here's what you'll do day-to-day:

  • Design and build across the Otto platform, including the runtime and agent graph, the tool/action layer, the Rails/GraphQL backend, and the advocate-facing experience, owning a meaningful slice end-to-end
  • Expand what the agent can safely do: autonomous, tool-using actions with human-in-the-loop approval, built on a secure, auditable identity and permissions model
  • Build the eval and observability systems (LLM-as-judge + deterministic scorers, offline gates and online production monitoring) that let us change the agent in production weekly without regressing correctness or escalation behavior
  • Work directly with LLMs, including prompting, tool/function design, retrieval, and the feedback loops that turn real advocate interactions into a better Otto
  • Partner closely with Product, Data, Design, and our CX teams to turn advocate and customer pain into shipped capabilities
  • Mentor engineers and set the bar for how we build agentic systems responsibly and at scale

Here's what we're looking for:

  • Typically 8+ years of software engineering experience, owning systems end-to-end
  • You've built and shipped LLM-powered or agentic systems in production, including tool-calling/agents, retrieval, or evals, and you know where they break and how to make them reliable
  • Strong full-stack range. Must be comfortable moving between backend services, an LLM runtime, and customer-facing product, and picking up whatever the problem needs
  • Experience owning multi-quarter projects that make measurable user impact at scale
  • Sound judgment about safety and blast radius when software takes real actions on real accounts
  • Ability to mentor engineers and raise the bar around you

Also great to have:

  • Experience with agent frameworks, evals/LLM-as-judge, retrieval, or MCP-style tool architectures
  • Experience with Ruby on Rails and/or JavaScript/React (our stack), or the ability to ramp quickly
  • Familiarity with GraphQL and federated backends
  • Appreciation for a collaborative, test-driven, high-autonomy environment, and clear communication with engineering and business stakeholders

At Gusto, we don't use titles the traditional way, as a signal of rank or hierarchy. Instead, our titles describe two things at once — your craft (what you do, e.g. Engineering, Design) and your scope of ownership (what you're accountable for) — which is why you'll see something like "Head of X" instead of a rank word like "Director" that tells you neither. Because of this, the title on this posting may look different from what you'd expect based on the scope and seniority of the role, and from how similar roles are typically titled elsewhere. If you have questions about how this role compares to your current title or level, your recruiter can help translate.
