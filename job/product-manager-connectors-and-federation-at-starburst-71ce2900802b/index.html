About Starburst

About the role

Starburst query engine workloads focus on two things: making federated queries fast across every source, and being the best place to run Apache Iceberg. Connectors and catalogs sit under both. They decide the breadth of connectors and use cases supported, how metadata is read, and how well Iceberg tables behave in practice.

AI agents reason across the whole data estate and generate queries as they go, so performance has to come from the engine and connectors working well on queries nobody planned in advance.

We are seeking a Product Manager to own the existing connector portfolio and catalog strategy, keeping both reliable, current, and prioritized by customer demand. You will also contribute to performance work on federation, including automated and dynamic approaches that engineering is building across data sources.

You will partner closely with other PMs to ramp up. We are looking for strong foundations and curiosity, not a perfect match on every item.

As Product Manager, Connectors and Federation at Starburst you will:

  • Own the roadmap and health of existing connectors, including database connectors and lake connectors such as Iceberg
  • Build new connectors where customer impact justifies it
  • Own the strategy for the flagship Starburst Metastore offering, along with ownership of other catalogs (HMS, Glue, REST catalogs such as Polaris, Unity Catalog, and others), with a focus on optionality rather than betting on one
  • Make federated queries faster by tracing slow queries to the responsible layer (engine, connector, or source) and turning findings into prioritized product work
  • Make Starburst the best place to run Iceberg by shaping read and write paths, table metadata handling, and catalog interoperability
  • Plan and prioritize performance enhancements for each connector based on customer demand, such as pushdown, type mapping, parallel reads, partition pruning, statistics, and metadata handling
  • Keep up to date with Trino spec changes and their impact on connectors and catalogs
  • Contribute to the automated, dynamic performance work across data sources, in partnership with engineering
  • Define what agents need from a federation layer beyond access, such as signaling partial visibility instead of returning a confident but incomplete answer
  • Prioritize connectors using customer demand evidence, revenue and churn context, and build and upkeep cost
  • Track the competitive field and community signals to inform prioritization

Some of the things we look for:

  • 3+ years as a Product Manager, with ownership of a technical infrastructure or data platform product
  • Hands on experience with a query engine: you have shipped or owned features on one, or built closely against one
  • Performance intuition: you ask for the plan before forming an opinion, and you can place a slowdown in the engine, the connector, or the source
  • Willingness to learn how connectors differ from one another, and to direct engineering toward the specific features that matter most for each
  • Working knowledge of object storage and the lakehouse, with a focus on Apache Iceberg
  • Working understanding of what a catalog does and the tradeoffs between HMS, Glue, and REST catalogs
  • Exposure to more than one source type, and awareness that type systems, limits, and consistency differ per source
  • Engineering credibility: you read docs, issues, and pull requests, and are comfortable with open source engine communities
  • Demand driven prioritization and clear written communication
  • Ability to Travel: This role will require 25% in-person travel for purposes including but not limited to new hire onboarding, team and department offsites, customer engagements, and other company events. Actual travel expectations may vary by role and business needs.

Added bonus, but not required:

  • Experience with Trino or another federated query engine
  • Deeper Iceberg knowledge, such as spec version differences and table maintenance (compaction, snapshot expiry)
  • Familiarity with authentication mechanisms such as OAuth
