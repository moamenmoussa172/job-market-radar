About the job

About the Institute of Foundation Models

The Institute of Foundation Models is a research lab at Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI) dedicated to building, understanding, and responsibly deploying foundation models. Our researchers and engineers work across model training, evaluation, and systems. We share our work through publications, technical reports, and open-source releases, and help train the next generation of AI researchers and builders.

Position Summary

The Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) team studies how foundation models can contribute to training and evaluating their successors. A model might construct a coding task, write a verifier, or use execution feedback to revise a solution. The research question is whether those contributions improve the next model’s ability to solve unfamiliar problems, and whether that model can, in turn, produce better training tasks and feedback.

As a Research Scientist, you will shape this research agenda and lead projects from an initial hypothesis through large-scale experiments. You will design learning algorithms, build environments that provide meaningful feedback, and study how gains and errors accumulate over successive training rounds. The role combines independent research with hands-on engineering across data generation, post-training, and evaluation.

Key Responsibilities

  • Develop algorithms for recursive self-improvement. Design and test methods for models to propose tasks, attempt solutions, learn from feedback, and select experiences for subsequent training rounds.
  • Build tasks and environments with verifiable outcomes. Develop agentic tasks in domains such as coding, program analysis, and tool use. Design execution-based verifiers and investigate what happens when a verifier accepts an incorrect solution or rejects a valid one.
  • Study how to select training data as models improve. Investigate which tasks produce the greatest learning gains at each stage of training and how to maintain coverage as earlier tasks become easier.
  • Measure improvement across generations. Design held-out evaluations, ablations, and compute-matched baselines that isolate the contribution of model-generated tasks and feedback. Diagnose failure modes such as reward hacking, declining task diversity, and errors that compound across rounds.
  • Scale promising methods. Work with research engineers to integrate generation, verification, and training into distributed post-training systems. Quantify the trade-offs among model capability, feedback quality, and computational cost.
  • Communicate and share research. Publish findings, contribute reproducible code and evaluations for public release, and build collaborations within MBZUAI and across the research community.
  • Academic Qualifications MSc, MEng, or PhD in machine learning, computer science, or a related field, or equivalent research experience.
