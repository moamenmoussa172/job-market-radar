About the job

Join the most advanced community of AI makers.

This role is in the ML & AI Applied Scientist archetype of the AI Applied Engineer chapter. Everyone in the chapter has the same title and the same foundation skills. Each person also has one main archetype, defined by the layer of the system they build, with skills specific to that archetype.

Mission

New models arrive every month, open or closed: you choose which ones are good enough for production, and you prove it. You build applications and agents on top of large language models (LLMs) and make the model part work. Our clients use these systems to answer customers, process documents and search their own knowledge.

You decide what context the model gets, how it retrieves knowledge and which tools it can call. You measure how good its answers are, and choose the model that fits the cost and quality target.

When an off-the-shelf model is not enough, you also build and adapt the models themselves: forecasting and optimisation engines, fine-tuned or distilled models, and the evaluation frameworks that prove a model is good enough.

This role is for you if you want to know whether an AI system gives correct answers, not only whether it runs.

Who you work with

Product Engineers build the application around the model; you own how the model behaves inside it. Context & Analytics Engineers prepare the knowledge your agents retrieve, and Platform Engineers own the shared harness, runtime and model-serving infrastructure; you own the agent logic and behaviour that runs on it.

With the AI Product Manager and the client's domain experts, you define what a good answer is and review results together. You review code with your peers and help newer colleagues.

What you do

  • Build machine learning applications, such as scoring or time series forecasting, with all the necessary steps: data wrangling, training loops, cross validation, evaluation, and deployment.
  • Build LLM applications and agents: context design, tool calling, multi-step workflows.
  • Design how agents use knowledge: retrieval queries, re-ranking, citations.
  • Build evaluation sets and pipelines: reference answers, automated scoring, review by domain experts.
  • Track answer quality in production and fix what degrades.
  • Choose models by quality, cost and latency. Fine-tune or distil a smaller model when prompting is not enough.
  • Add guardrails against wrong answers, data leaks and prompt injection.
  • Process documents with LLMs: extraction, classification, summaries.
  • Frame the modelling problem with the AI Product Manager and the business: the objective, the constraints and the metric that matters.
  • Build forecasting and optimisation engines, and take them to production with Platform Engineers.
  • Design evaluation frameworks and benchmarks for models and agents, including reinforcement learning where it helps.
  • Run model research and benchmarking, and turn the results into reusable assets for the chapter.
  • Explain model behaviour, uncertainty and limits to business stakeholders.

Required Skills

Must-haves:

  • You have built an application or agent that calls an LLM, and you can explain where it worked and where it failed.
  • You write tested, maintainable code.
  • You already use coding agents in your daily work and check what they produce.
  • You measure AI output quality with test sets and metrics, and you know how to limit made-up answers, prompt injection and data leaks.
  • You know what it takes to put an LLM application in production and keep it there: deployment, monitoring, cost and latency.
  • You start from the business problem, not the model, and you can explain to non-engineers when AI is not the right tool.
  • You have a solid foundation in machine learning and statistics: you can explain why a model works and when it will fail.

Bonus, not required:

  • Fine-tuning or distillation of language models.
  • Agent frameworks or protocols such as MCP.
  • Multimodal inputs: documents, images, speech.
  • LLM observability tools: tracing, cost tracking.
  • Forecasting, optimisation or reinforcement learning in production.
  • Publications, patents or open-source models.

You don't need to tick every box to apply. If you match most must-haves and want to learn the rest, we want to hear from you.

Tech grid skills that matter most: ML, Optimization & Foundation Models; Applied Math, Statistics & AI Evaluation; AI & Agentic Solutions.

Certifications (a plus): Databricks Machine Learning Professional, Google Cloud Professional Machine Learning Engineer.

Interview focus: Technical interview: maths, ML and LLM evaluation fundamentals. System design: go deep on the model or agent behaviour and how you evaluate it. Hands-on build: an LLM or ML component for the case and the evaluation that proves it works.

Foundation skills, whatever your archetype

  • Software craft: write code that is tested, reviewed and documented.
  • Production ownership: own what you ship after launch, including monitoring, incidents and fixes.
  • AI evaluation: define and run evaluations for AI features, and say when AI is not the right tool.
  • AI system security basics: manage access rights and secrets, and guard against prompt injection.
  • AI-assisted development: write the specification, delegate to coding agents, review the result and keep token costs under control.
  • Product and business sense: build for users, ship a first version, measure adoption, improve it.
  • Client relationship: explain your choices to clients and manage your part of the project.

What we expect at your level

  • Senior: owns a workstream of their archetype end to end, splits and estimates the work, reviews code and works autonomously with the client.

What to expect / Recruitment process

The process has five interviews. You don't need to choose an archetype before applying: after the HR interview, interviews 2 to 4 are run by engineers from the archetype that fits your experience, with a case from that archetype. Each archetype's interview focus is described in its section above.

  • HR interview (45 min): your background and current situation, your fit (autonomy, client posture, teamwork) and your interest in building AI systems.
  • Technical interview (60 min) with an engineer from your archetype: the fundamentals of your archetype (live coding for Product and Context & Analytics, cloud and DevOps for Platform, maths, ML and LLM evaluation for ML & AI Applied Scientists), then a deep dive on something you built.
  • System design (60 min) with two engineers from different archetypes: design the AI system for a client use case, go deep on your part (application, platform, data and context, or model) and defend your choices.
  • Hands-on build (60 min) with an engineer from your archetype: build a working piece of the previous case with the tools of your choice, AI coding assistants included, then demo it and walk through it.
  • Fit interview (30 min) with a Director: meet the team and learn how we work.
