About the Role:

The AI SOC team builds autonomous security operations into the CrowdStrike Falcon platform. When a detection fires, our AI agents triage it, group it with related detections and investigate the result end to end. Specialist agents examine endpoint, identity, third-party and log data. A lead agent weighs the evidence and gives a verdict that analysts can audit. The system then recommends or takes response actions, with human approval, and writes the final report. The product runs in production with early customers and will soon reach general availability. An AI-native SOC is a central part of CrowdStrike’s AI strategy.

We look for a technically strong, product oriented engineering manager to lead a team of 10–12 experienced engineers and SDETs. With your team, you make sure that the agents give correct verdicts, that the system runs reliably at scale and stays within its cost budget, and that security analysts can trust it to act for them. You must be able to review designs, challenge architecture and trade-offs, and contribute to deep technical discussions about distributed systems and LLM agents.

The team works in two-week sprints with a “you build it, you run it” ownership model. You partner very closely with product managers, with the Falcon Complete security analysts who define how a good investigation is done, with the Charlotte AI and GenAI platform teams, and with data scientists. You also work with ProdSec, InfoSec, Legal and Privacy to ship secure, compliant, enterprise-grade capabilities. Teams and partners are in Europe and the US.

What You’ll Do:

  • Lead an AI SOC engineering team. Own planning and delivery for a major area of the product, for example the agentic investigation engine or AI detection triage
  • Provide technical oversight. Review designs, API contracts and architecture. Make sure that the team has a high bar for testing, code review and design discussion. Ask the questions that find risks early, and know when to bring in experts.
  • Own the quality of AI outcomes. Drive benchmarks and evaluations of the agents. Define success metrics (verdict accuracy, false positives and false negatives, time to verdict, cost per investigation). Turn analyst and customer feedback into measurable improvements.
  • Get the product ready for GA and keep it healthy. SLOs, telemetry, dashboards and alerts, the on-call rotation, incident response and postmortems, staged rollouts with change management, load tests and autoscaling.
  • Manage cost and scale. Keep LLM usage, cost per investigation, latency and customer credit limits visible and under control as usage grows.
  • Build trust in autonomy. Make sure that agents act safely as autonomy increases: human-in-the-loop approval for response actions, clear policies, and a full audit trail of agent decisions and evidence.
  • Deliver new capabilities. With product management, turn new customer problems (for example investigation memory, more data sources and more autonomous response) into clear problem statements, spikes and a delivery plan.
  • Run the team’s processes. Sprint planning with discovery spikes, 1:1s, performance reviews, bug triage, customer escalations and retrospectives.
  • Shape the roadmap. Co-create a 6–12 month roadmap with product managers and engineering directors, sequence work across teams, and land on timelines when priorities change.
  • Communicate clearly. Report status, risks, decisions and trade-offs to engineers, partners and leadership.

What You'll Need:

  • 8+ years in software engineering and 2+ years as an engineering manager, including a team of 8 or more.
  • Technical depth to evaluate the design of distributed cloud systems: event-driven pipelines, workflow orchestration, data stores and search, multi-tenancy, permissions. You are comfortable reading code in reviews.
  • A record of leading teams that build and operate production services at meaningful scale, with strong on-call, SLO and postmortem habits.
  • Cloud-native experience: containers, Kubernetes and AWS (or another major cloud). Pragmatic about reliability, latency and cost.
  • A working knowledge of LLM-based systems: agents and tool use, context engineering, evaluation, and their failure modes (non-determinism, hallucination, cost). Or clear proof that you learn new technical domains quickly.
  • Product sense: you can turn customer and business needs into crisp problem statements, success metrics and priorities, and you partner well with product managers.
  • People-first leadership: an empowering, supportive and empathetic style that brings out the best in experienced engineers, also at a fast pace with changing priorities.
  • Comfort with ambiguity: a bias for action and the ability to make decisions with incomplete information.
  • Excellent written and verbal communication in English.
  • Experience working across geographies and time zones, with the flexibility to attend meetings with partners in the US.

Bonus Points:

  • Security operations domain knowledge: SOC workflows, SIEM, EDR/XDR, incident response or managed detection and response.
  • Experience shipping AI features that take actions for users, with guardrails, human approval and auditability.
  • Familiarity with our stack: Go, Temporal, Kafka, OpenSearch, Redis, Kubernetes on AWS.
  • Exposure to agent frameworks, evaluation and benchmarking tooling, RAG or agent memory in production.
  • A security-first mindset and experience with privacy and compliance requirements for enterprise systems.
