About the job

Do you want to build the system that tells millions of engineers the moment something goes wrong? As a Software Development Engineer on Amazon CloudWatch Alarms, you will design and operate the service that continuously evaluates customer metrics and logs, and acts on them in real time — notifying on-call teams, scaling fleets, and recovering resources, often while the rest of a customer's infrastructure is under stress. When our customers have an incident, our alarms have to be right and on time — that is the problem you will work on every day.
CloudWatch is the observability service of Amazon Web Services (AWS), used by developers, site reliability engineers (SREs), system operators, and IT managers to monitor their applications and infrastructure. CloudWatch processes quadrillions of metric observations every month and continuosly evaluates millions of alarms, with tight latency and correctness requirements.
This role is based in Dublin, Ireland.
Key job responsibilities
Responsibilities include, but are not limited to:
Design, build and operate highly available distributed systems that evaluate customer alarms and trigger notifications and automated actions across AWS.
Deliver new alarming capabilities — from composite alarms to anomaly detection — from design through launch in every AWS Region.
Dive deep into operational issues, identify root causes, and put fixes in place that prevent them from recurring.
Collaborate with partner teams across CloudWatch and AWS to turn customer requirements into designs and incremental, safe deliverables.
Mentor junior engineers and share what you learn through design and code reviews.
A day in the life
You might start the day investigating why a customer's alarm changed state later than expected, then join a design review for a new capability that lets customers monitor thousands of resources with a single alarm. In the afternoon, you could profile the evaluation path to cut latency and cost. AI coding assistants and agents are part of how you work every day — you'll use them to explore unfamiliar code, prototype ideas, write tests, and speed up investigations, freeing up more of your time for design and hard problems.
This role includes participating in an on-call rotation for the services the team owns. We don't like getting paged in the middle of the night or on the weekend, so we build our systems to be fault tolerant. When we do get paged, we work together to resolve the root cause so we don't get paged for the same issue twice.
About the team
CloudWatch Alarms is how AWS customers learn that something needs their attention. Our customers range from startups to the largest enterprises, and teams across Amazon rely on our alarms to keep their own services healthy. Our vision is for every alarm to be timely, accurate and actionable at any scale. Over the next few years we're focused on making alarms easier to set up across large, changing environments and smarter about what truly needs a human's attention — and you will help shape that roadmap.
Our team has a broad mix of experience levels and Amazon tenures, and we've built an environment that celebrates knowledge sharing and mentorship. Our senior engineers enjoy mentoring engineers at every stage, including those from non-traditional backgrounds, through one-on-one mentoring and thorough, but kind, code reviews. We assign projects based on what will help you grow into a well-rounded engineer, so you can take on more complex work over time.
- Experience (non-internship) in professional software development
- Experience designing or architecting (design patterns, reliability and scaling) of new and existing systems
- Hands on programming skills in at least one of the following: Rust, Go, Java, C++, Python
- Bachelor's degree in computer science or equivalent
- Experience with full software development life cycle, including coding standards, code reviews, source control management, build processes, testing, and operations
- Open-source enthusiasm and contributions (e.g. a GitHub portfolio, or contributions to projects such as OpenTelemetry)
