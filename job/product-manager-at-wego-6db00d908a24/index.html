About the job

This is a fully remote job, open to people in Indonesia, Vietnam.

About Wego

Wego is the leading travel app and the largest online travel marketplace in the Middle East and North Africa. Our mission is to make travel better by helping millions of people discover, compare, and book flights, hotels, and travel experiences with confidence.

Founded in 2005, Wego has grown from a metasearch engine into a full travel platform, combining search with our own OTA products. We don't just point travelers at a fare: we sell it, ticket it, and stand behind it when plans change.

That means we own the whole transaction: the checkout, everything we sell alongside the ticket, and the tools our support agents use when a traveler needs help. This role works across all three.

Role summary

You will own product work across three areas of our flights booking business: new ancillary services, booking funnel optimisation, and improvements to the Customer Support Admin Tool our support agents use every day. You will join a team of product managers, including a Senior Product Manager who leads our post-booking work, and you will take features from problem definition to launch and measured result.

This is a product execution role. We care more about how well you take a problem from definition to a measured outcome than about how many years you have spent in travel. Flights experience helps, and you will learn the domain fast here, because the people around you know it deeply and the problems are concrete.

Key responsibilities

Feature ownership. Own features and initiatives from problem statement to launch and measured result. Make the product calls within your scope, and know when to escalate.

Specification. Write clear PRDs, user stories, decision tables and acceptance criteria that engineers can build from without a meeting. Cover the edge cases, the failure paths, and what we will not do.

Delivery with engineering and design. Work daily with engineering and design on scope, sequencing and trade-offs. Unblock decisions quickly. Keep scope honest when timelines slip.

Experimentation. Design and run A/B tests on the booking funnel and on ancillary offers. Define one primary metric and guardrails up front, test one change at a time where you can, and report results you would defend.

Testing and launch. Define test scenarios with QA and operations, run acceptance testing, plan staged rollouts with clear go/no-go criteria, and check that it works in production, not just in staging.

Measurement. Define the success metric for every feature before it is built. Instrument it, read the data yourself, and report what moved and why.

Partnership with operations and commercial. Work with customer support, finance and commercial teams, and with ancillary partners. Make sure every launch includes what support agents need to handle it.

Communication. Keep stakeholders informed with short written updates: what shipped, what it did, what's next, and what is blocked.

Who you are

Required

  • 3+ years in product management, with direct ownership of features that shipped to customers or operational users.
  • A strong execution record. You can point to features you took from a messy problem to a measured outcome, and explain the trade-offs you made along the way.
  • Hands-on experimentation. You have designed and read A/B tests yourself, and you know the common ways they mislead.
  • Excellent written specifications. Your PRDs and acceptance criteria are precise, cover edge cases, and hold up when engineers build from them.
  • Analytical and hands-on with data. You write your own queries (SQL or equivalent), build the dashboard you need, and change your mind on evidence.
  • Experience building for operational users, such as support agents, fulfillment or back-office teams, or a clear interest in doing so.
  • Clear, direct communication with technical and non-technical audiences, and the confidence to raise a problem early.

Preferred

  • Experience in travel, ideally flights: OTA, airline, GDS or travel-tech.
  • E-commerce checkout, payments or conversion optimization.
  • Ancillary or add-on products: insurance, protection, upsells, bundles.
  • Internal tools for customer support or operations, such as agent consoles or case management.

This is probably not the role for you if

  • You prefer setting strategy to getting the details of a launch right.
  • You'd rather hand testing, experiment analysis and post-launch checks to someone else.
  • You see internal tools as less important than customer-facing products.
