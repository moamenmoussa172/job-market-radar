About the job

Ready to do the most impactful work of your career? Coinbase is building the future of finance for people, institutions and their agents, with one place to trade crypto, equities, derivatives and prediction markets, use stablecoins, and connect AI agents with clear controls. The bar is high and the pace is intense, and we like it that way. This isn’t a place for complacency. It’s a place to be pushed past your perceived limits and do the best work of your career. If you’re ready to build the future of finance with a championship team who refuse to settle for “good enough,” you belong here. Coinbase is a remote-first, but not remote-only company. Expect to get together quarterly for in-person working sessions

This is a 12-week internship during summer 2027.

Coinbase’s Application Security team is rethinking how offensive security works. We use frontier AI models to find vulnerabilities at scale, red team our own AI systems, and automate the parts of security work that don't need a human. As an intern, you'll join that effort on a scoped project and see your work used in real security decisions.

What you'll do:

  • Build and test tooling that uses frontier AI models to surface vulnerabilities across Coinbase’s products, working alongside senior engineers on design and review.
  • Help red team internal AI systems - probing for prompt injection, jailbreak attempts, and tool misuse, then documenting what you find and how to fix it.
  • Automate pieces of the vulnerability triage, validation and remediation workflows so the bug bounty, vulnerability response pipelines move faster and become AI native.
  • Reproduce and verify reported vulnerabilities across common classes (OWASP Top 10, SANS Top 25), and write findings that engineering partners can act on.
  • Practice responsible AI use: keep a human in the loop, measure whether your tooling actually improves speed or quality, and be honest when it doesn’t.

Required Skills and Experience:

  • Currently pursuing a degree or certificate in Computer Science, Software Engineering, Mathematics or a related area discipline.
  • Proficiency in Python or Go and strong software development skills shown through coursework, personal projects and internships.
  • Curiosity about LLMs and a willingness to experiment: you’ve at least tried building with a model API and thought about how it could be manipulated.
  • Experience with security tooling such as Burp Suite, Semgrep, fuzzing frameworks, or Metasploit.
  • Hands-on exploration of prompt injection or jailbreak techniques.
  • Clear communication, especially in writing - you can explain a technical finding to someone who has to fix it.
  • Experience with GitHub and AWS.
