About the job

We are looking for an experienced, hands-on BOM Engineer to own the product structure of RIVR's robots from start to finish. That runs from the engineering BOM our hardware team designs, to the manufacturing BOM our in-house manufacturing needs, to the procurement, service and trade data our operations depend on. You will make our PLM the single source of truth for what goes into every robot: every part, revision, supplier and change.
Our hardware engineers own the design content of the BOM. You own its structure, accuracy and release process, across every BOM type. You will be RIVR's business owner and lead user of the PLM, working with IT teams to bring RIVR into Amazon's systems. You will also own the engineering change process from start to finish: every change gets reviewed, released and carried through by every team it affects, from supply chain and manufacturing to trade, logistics and field operations.
This is a builder role. RIVR is moving from startup tooling to Amazon-scale systems, and our product portfolio will grow. You will design how we structure, release and change product data in a way that scales across product lines, not inherit a mature process. At the same time, this is a cross-functional role, your job is to bring teams together and to enable them to work effectively together, not to add bureaucracy.
Key job responsibilities
- Product data ownership & PLM: Act as business owner and lead user of RIVR's product data in our PLM. Define part numbering, item master, attributes, lifecycle states, revision rules, and structure for variants and multiple products. Turn RIVR's needs into requirements for the PLM system team, which owns system configuration and administration. Lead RIVR's migration from current tools into our new PLM, and hold the quality bar for the data afterwards.
- BOM structure & release (all BOM types): Own how every BOM type is structured, checked and released. Set release gates for new parts: approved supplier, datasheets, and compliance and trade data in place before release.
- Engineering change management (end to end): Own the engineering change process (ECR/ECO) from request to closure. Anyone in engineering or operations can request a change. Hardware engineers own the technical solution and design. You run impact analysis with every affected function, organize reviews, set effectivity, release in PLM, and close a change only once every downstream team has acted on it: supply chain, manufacturing, trade & product compliance, logistics, field operations, software.
- Engineering BOM (eBOM): Work with hardware engineers to structure and release eBOMs from mechanical CAD, electrical CAD and harness design. Keep the eBOM correct through every build phase, from prototype to mass production.
- Manufacturing BOM (mBOM): With manufacturing engineering, turn eBOMs into mBOMs for our in-house production as we scale: assembly structure, consumables, kitting, tooling and alternate parts. Prepare BOM and routing data for handoff to MES and ERP. Keep eBOM and mBOM in sync, and make every unit traceable by serial and lot number back to the BOM it was built to.
- Procurement BOM (pBOM): Work with supply chain on clean BOMs for sourcing and costing: approved manufacturer/vendor lists, lead times, alternates and second sources, and costed BOM rollups. Track part lifecycle and obsolescence (end-of-life notices, last-time buys).
- Service BOM and field operations (“sBOM”): With field operations, define and maintain the service BOM: spare parts and field-replaceable units for deployed robots. Help maintain the as-built configuration of the fleet so field teams always know what's in each robot, and what revision of spare parts needs to be order for each robot and where backwards compatibility applies.
- Trade & product compliance (“cBOM”): Make sure every part carries the data trade and product compliance need: country of origin, supplier origin, technical inputs for HTS/ECCN classification, and material declarations (RoHS, REACH, conflict minerals, lithium batteries). Trade owns classification. Product compliance owns material and certification decisions. You own complete, accurate part data and make sure every relevant change triggers the right review.
- Hardware/software configuration: With the software team, link firmware and software releases to hardware configurations so every robot build has a defined, traceable hardware + software compatibility. The software team owns the software BOM (SBOM). You make sure it connects to the hardware BOM.
- Processes & metrics: Write the SOPs, templates and training that make BOM and change discipline the default across engineering and operations. Own and report KPIs: BOM accuracy, ECO cycle time, % of released parts with complete data, and build or shipping issues caused by BOM errors.
About the team
RIVR, part of Amazon is a robotics company pioneering Physical AI through real-world doorstep delivery. Founded in 2024 as an ETH Zurich spin-off, RIVR developed wheeled-legged robots designed to operate in complex, unstructured environments such as stairs, gates, doors, and uneven urban terrain. We believe that achieving general physical intelligence requires solving real customer problems in the real world, where robots can learn from rich operational data at scale.
Following our acquisition by Amazon in March 2026, we are continuing this mission with greater reach and speed. By combining custom robot hardware, onboard autonomy, and cloud-based coordination, RIVR, part of Amazon is building the next generation of safe, reliable autonomous robots for last-mile delivery.
